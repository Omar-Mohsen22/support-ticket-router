# Step 05: few-shot prompting notes

Two variants of `src/ticket_router/predictors/api_predictor.py` (`--variant coverage` and
`--variant coverage-priority`), same model (gpt-4o-mini), same full test split (1,633 rows), same
harness. Exactly two variants, as instructed -- this is a measurement of whether prompting closes a
gap, not a search for the best prompt.

## What each variant changed

**Variant A (`coverage`)**: 10 few-shot examples added to every prompt as prior user/assistant
turns, one per queue class. Selection rule (deterministic, in code --
`select_few_shot_examples()`): for each of the 10 queue classes, the training row with the
**shortest `text`** (ties broken by lowest `id`), to keep the added prompt small. The 10 examples
actually used are saved in full at `results/few_shot_examples.json` (regenerated by the script every
run, not hand-copied here). One example is worth flagging: the shortest `Human Resources` example is
*"Can you provide details on integrating data analytics with FL Studio?"* -- a synthetic-data
labeling artifact (FL Studio is DAW/music software, not HR), not a selection bug. The rule was
followed exactly as specified; it wasn't cherry-picked around this.

**Variant B (`coverage-priority`)**: Variant A's 10 examples, plus a one-sentence description per
`priority` value inserted into the system prompt. Derivation (`derive_priority_definitions()`, fully
reproducible, saved to `results/priority_definitions.json` with both the sentences and the raw
ranked word lists): for each priority value, a Laplace-smoothed relative-word-frequency ratio
against the training tickets of the *other* two values (a keyness/log-odds-style analysis),
restricted to words occurring in at least 20 training tickets of that value. The three derived
sentences (verbatim, as sent to the model):

- **high**: severe, timeouts, major, reverting, rolling, overloads, outage, reliability,
  interruption, ceased, interrupted, capacity
- **low**: com, lack, providers, browser, feature, low, commitments, finds, utmost, value, long,
  delay
- **medium**: demonstrate, scope, app, organizations, modifying, knowing, transaction, lately,
  compatible, month, predictions, conflicting

No interpretation or paraphrase was added on top of these word lists -- the prompt states them as
the empirical finding they are. `high`'s list reads as a coherent "outage/severe-failure" signal.
`low`'s and `medium`'s lists are visibly noisier (`com` is very likely a URL/domain tokenization
artifact, not a substantive signal) -- reported as found, not smoothed over.

## Effect on macro-F1 per field

| run | queue F1 | priority F1 | type F1 | exact-match | invalid-rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero-shot (step-04) | 0.2554 | 0.3214 | 0.5209 | 10.90% | 0.00% |
| **A: coverage** (10 few-shot) | 0.2442 | 0.3135 | 0.4319 | 6.86% | 0.37% |
| **B: coverage-priority** (A + priority defs) | 0.2241 | 0.3224 | 0.4710 | 6.92% | 2.02% |
| tfidf-baseline (reference, step-04b) | 0.3683 | 0.5445 | 0.8412 | 29.33% | 0.00% |
| random-baseline (reference, step-03) | 0.1110 | 0.3427 | 0.2577 | 2.20% | 0.00% |

(Full per-class breakdowns for every run: `results/comparison.md`, `results/results.json`.)

**Few-shot prompting made `queue` and `type` worse, not better**, in both variants. `queue`
macro-F1 fell monotonically: 0.2554 -> 0.2442 -> 0.2241. `type` collapsed from 0.5209 to 0.4319 in
Variant A and only partially recovered to 0.4710 in Variant B, still well below zero-shot. 10
single-example-per-class demonstrations, with no coverage of ambiguous or borderline cases, appear
to have narrowed the model's behavior rather than improved it -- plausibly by anchoring it toward
the specific (short, sometimes atypical) examples shown, at the cost of generalization.

**`priority` barely moved at all**: 0.3214 -> 0.3135 (Variant A, slightly worse) -> 0.3224 (Variant
B, +0.0010 over zero-shot -- not a meaningful change). Even with explicit, data-derived priority
definitions placed directly in the prompt, the model's `priority` macro-F1 stayed statistically
indistinguishable from the 0.3427 random-baseline reference, and nowhere near TF-IDF's 0.5445.

## Which errors persisted

**The single most important finding**: `priority = low` gets **zero recall (0 / 345) in all three
LLM variants** -- zero-shot, coverage, and coverage-priority alike. The model never once predicts
`low`, regardless of prompting. Handing it the empirically-derived words most associated with `low`
tickets in training data (Variant B) did not change this at all. This is not a coverage-of-examples
problem (Variant A already included one `low`-priority-adjacent case implicitly across its 10
queue-class examples) and not a missing-information problem (Variant B stated the defining
vocabulary outright) -- the model appears to have a structural bias against predicting `low`
regardless of what's in the prompt.

**Secondary finding, operational not qualitative**: invalid-output rate rose with prompt size --
0.00% (zero-shot) -> 0.37% (Variant A) -> 2.02% (Variant B). Every one of these failures (6 in A, 33
in B) was a `RateLimitError` (429, tokens-per-minute), confirmed by inspecting every failed row's
error field -- **zero were JSON-parse or schema-validation failures** in either variant. Longer
prompts (more few-shot examples, then also the priority-definitions paragraph) mean more tokens
consumed per request, which under `CONCURRENCY_LIMIT = 5` pushed the run closer to the account's
per-minute token budget more often. This is an infrastructure side-effect of larger prompts, not a
sign the model was confused by them -- reported here for completeness, not folded into the
macro-F1 numbers as if it were a content-quality issue (`evaluate.py` correctly still counts these
rows as invalid/wrong, since a missing prediction is a missing prediction regardless of cause).

## Verdict: did few-shot prompting close the gap to TF-IDF?

**No. Not on any field, and prompting made two of the three fields worse.**

| field | zero-shot -> best few-shot variant | tfidf-baseline | gap closed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| queue | 0.2554 -> 0.2442 (A) | 0.3683 | No -- moved further away |
| priority | 0.3214 -> 0.3224 (B) | 0.5445 | No -- +0.0010, not a real change |
| type | 0.5209 -> 0.4710 (B) | 0.8412 | No -- moved further away |

Step-04c's leakage check already established that TF-IDF's advantage is genuine signal, not an
artifact of near-duplicate leakage. This step adds the second half of that conclusion: two rounds of
prompting -- including one that handed the model empirically-derived, data-grounded priority
definitions directly -- did not recover any of that gap. Whatever TF-IDF is picking up on is not
something either of these two prompt variants successfully communicated to the LLM. That's a
finding about the prompting approach tried here, not proof that no prompt could ever work --
per the step-05 instruction, this stops at two variants and does not chase a better one.
